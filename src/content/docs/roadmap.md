GATE 2027 runs on **6, 7, 13, 14, 20 and 21 February 2027** (organised by IIT Madras). The DA syllabus is unchanged from 2026, so all three past papers still apply.

## Order of subjects on this site

Non-maths subjects come first, as you asked. They are ordered by how stable and heavy they were in 2024–2026.

| # | Subject | Why this position |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **PDSA** | Most stable subject in the paper (17 / 16 / 16). Python tracing comes up every year. |
| 2 | Machine Learning | Rising every year (11 → 14 → 16). Metrics and overfitting questions are near-certain. |
| 3 | DBMS & Warehousing | Spiked to 18 in 2026. Normalization and SQL come up every year. |
| 4 | Artificial Intelligence | Volatile (3–11), but A\* and logic questions are cheap marks. |
| 5 | Probability & Statistics | Largest maths block. Bayes comes up every year. |
| 6 | Linear Algebra | Eigenvalues come up every year. Links to PCA and SVD. |
| 7 | Calculus & Optimization | Small (7–8) but always present. |
| 8 | General Aptitude | 15 fixed marks. Do 30 minutes of PYQs daily alongside everything else. |

## How to study one topic page (about 2 hours)

1. **Read** the concepts and formula box (40 min). Don't skip the "from zero" parts, even if they look easy.
2. **Solve** each worked PYQ yourself *before* opening the solution (40 min).
3. **Practice**: do the practice set at the end, then the matching section of `question-bank.pdf` in that subject's folder (`subjects/<subject>/`) (30 min).
4. **Close the page** and rewrite the formula box from memory (10 min).
