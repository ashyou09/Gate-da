**Why it matters:** an analogy appeared in **every** paper (2024 Q1, 2025 Q1, 2026 Q1), and 2025 Q6 tested phrasal verbs. They're quick 1–2 mark questions, provided you **name the relationship** before you look at the options.

## Topic map

<div class="mindmap">

- **Vocabulary**
  - Analogy relationships
    - synonym, antonym, degree / intensity
    - part–whole, cause–effect, tool–use, worker–product
  - Method
    - make a sentence linking the first pair

  - Confusable words
    - vain / vein / vane, affect / effect, principal / principle
  - Phrasal verbs
    - bring up, take up, clear up, look up, put off, call off

## 2.1 The analogy method

1. **State the relationship** of the first pair in one sentence. "Courage *means the same as* bravery."
2. **Apply the same sentence** to the third word and each option. "Yearning *means the same as* ___ → longing."
3. If two options fit, make the sentence **more specific** (degree, direction, part of speech).

| Relationship | Example pair |
|---|---|
| Synonym | courage : bravery, yearning : longing |
| **Antonym** | verbosity : brevity, insolence : respect |
| **Degree (increasing intensity)** | sick → infirm → moribund; silly → vain → daft |
| Part : whole | page : book |
| Cause : effect | virus : infection |
| Worker : product | author : novel |
| Tool : use | scalpel : surgeon |

**Watch for spelling traps.** Options often include a **homophone**: *vain* (foolishly proud or useless) vs *vein* (a blood vessel) vs *vane* (a weather vane). Only one fits the meaning.

## 2.2 Commonly confused words

| Pair | Meaning |
|---|---|
| affect / effect | verb "to influence" / noun "result" |
| principal / principle | the main one, or the head of a school / a rule or belief |
| complement / compliment | to complete / to praise |
| stationary / stationery | not moving / writing material |
| discreet / discrete | tactful / separate |
| eminent / imminent | distinguished / about to happen |
| elicit / illicit | draw out / illegal |
| loath / loathe | unwilling / to hate |

## Summary: important points

<div class="formula">

1. Name the relationship first, then test each option with the same sentence.
2. The common types are synonym, antonym, **degree**, part–whole and cause–effect.
3. In a degree analogy, the blank is the **middle** intensity of the same meaning family.
4. Watch for homophones (vain / vein) and confusable pairs.
5. Phrasal verbs: bring up = raise a topic, take up = pursue a matter with someone, clear up = tidy, look up = search.

</div>

## Test yourself (A): previous-year questions

### GATE 2024 Q1 (1 mark)

If '→' means increasing intensity, then [sick → infirm → moribund] is analogous to [silly → ____ → daft].
(A) frown (B) fawn (C) vein (D) vain

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (D) vain**

Silly → vain (foolish) → daft (very foolish) is a scale of foolishness. *Vein* is the spelling trap, and *frown* and *fawn* are unrelated.

</details>

### GATE 2025 Q1 (1 mark)

Courage : Bravery :: Yearning : ____
(A) Longing (B) Yelling (C) Yawning (D) Glaring

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (A)**

The relationship is synonymy. The other options are distractors that sound or look similar.

</details>

### GATE 2026 Q1 (1 mark)

Verbosity : Brevity :: Insolence : ____
(A) Innocence (B) Respect (C) Solace (D) Wealth

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (B)**

The relationship is antonymy. Insolence means disrespectful rudeness, so its opposite is respect.

</details>

### GATE 2025 Q6 (2 marks)

Statements: P "This house is in a mess." Q "I am not happy with the marks given to me." R "Politics is a subject I avoid talking about." S "I don't know what this word means."
Responses: 1 "I won't bring it up during our conversations." 2 "You can easily look it up." 3 "Let me clear it up for you." 4 "I will take it up with your teacher."

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (B)** P–3, Q–4, R–1, S–2

Mess → clear it up. Marks complaint → take it up with the teacher. Avoided topic → won't bring it up. Unknown word → look it up.

</details>

## Test yourself (B): predicted 2027-style questions

**F1.** Drizzle : Downpour :: Whisper : ____
(A) Murmur (B) Shout (C) Silence (D) Speak

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (B)**

This is a degree analogy: light → heavy rain, so soft → loud speech.

</details>

**F2.** Author : Novel :: Sculptor : ____
(A) Chisel (B) Statue (C) Museum (D) Marble

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (B)**

The relationship is worker : product. The chisel (A) is a tool and marble (D) is a material.

</details>

**F3.** Choose the word that fits: "The ____ of the new policy will be felt next year."
(A) affect (B) effect (C) efect (D) affection

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (B)**

A noun meaning "result" is needed.

</details>

**F4.** "The meeting has been ____ because the chairperson is ill." (postponed to a later date)
(A) called off (B) put off (C) turned down (D) given up

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (B)**

"Put off" means postpone. "Called off" would mean cancelled.

</details>
