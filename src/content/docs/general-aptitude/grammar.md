## 1.1 Tenses: the 3 × 4 grid

| | Simple | Continuous | Perfect | Perfect continuous |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Past** | played | was playing | had played | **had been playing** |
| **Present** | play(s) | am/is/are playing | has/have played | has/have been playing |
| **Future** | will play | will be playing | will have played | will have been playing |

- **Continuous:** in progress at a reference time.
- **Perfect:** completed before a reference time.
- **Perfect continuous:** started before a reference time **and still going on** at it.

**Sequence of tenses:** if the main clause is in the past, the other verbs usually shift into the past as well. "She said she **was** tired", not "is". Use the past perfect for the earlier of two past events: "By the time we arrived, the film **had started**."

## 1.2 Conditionals

<div class="formula">

| Type | Form | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Zero | If + present, present | general truth: "If you heat ice, it melts." |
| First | If + present, will + verb | real future possibility |
| Second | If + past, would + verb | unreal present: "If I were rich, I would…" |
| **Third** | If + **had** + past participle, **would have** + past participle | unreal **past**: the condition did **not** happen |

</div>

"If his movie **had been** a success, he **would have** made money" implies the movie was **not** a success. That's 2026 Q5, covered in [reading & inference](/general-aptitude/reading-inference/).

## 1.3 Prepositions

| Use | Preposition | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Point in time | **at** | at 5 pm, at night |
| Day or date | **on** | on Monday, on 26 January |
| Month, year, longer period | **in** | in May, in 2027, in the morning |
| Duration | **for** | for the night, for three days |
| Starting point | **since** | since 2020 |
| Deadline | **by** | by Friday |
| A specific point or place | **at** | at the door, at the station |
| Inside an area | **in** | in the cabin, in Delhi |
| On a surface | **on** | on the table |
| Belonging, part of | **of** | the door **of** the cabin |

"Parted **at** the door **of** the cabin that I had rented **for** the night" (2026 Q6): at a specific point, part of the cabin, for a duration.

## 1.4 Articles

- **a / an** is chosen by the **sound** of the next word: *an hour*, *an MBA*, *a university*, *a one-time offer*.
- **the** marks something **specific** or **unique**: *the sun*, *the book I lent you*, superlatives (*the best*), and rivers, oceans and plural country names (*the Ganga*, *the USA*).
- **No article** for general plurals or uncountables: "*Water* is essential".

## 1.5 Subject–verb agreement

- The verb agrees with the **real subject**, not the nearest noun: "The quality of the answers **is** high."
- **Each, every, either, neither, everyone, none (formal)** take a singular verb: "Each of the students **has**…"
- With **either…or** or **neither…nor**, the verb agrees with the **nearer** subject: "Neither the teacher nor the students **were**…"
- Collective nouns (team, committee) are usually singular in Indian and British exam English unless the members act separately.

---

## Summary: important points

<div class="formula">

1. Perfect continuous means started earlier and **still going on** at the reference time: *had been playing … when it started to rain*.
2. Past perfect is for the earlier of two past events.
3. Third conditional (*if … had …, would have …*) means the condition did **not** happen.
4. Prepositions: **at** a point, **on** a day or surface, **in** a period or enclosed space, **for** a duration, **since** a starting point.
5. a or an goes by **sound**.
6. The verb agrees with the head noun. Each, every and either take a singular verb.

</div>

## Test yourself (A): previous-year questions

### GATE 2025 Q2 (1 mark)

"We __________ tennis in the lawn when it suddenly started to rain."
(A) have been playing (B) had been playing (C) would have been playing (D) could be playing

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (B)**

The playing was in progress up to a past moment (the rain), which calls for the past perfect continuous. (A) is the present perfect continuous, which can't sit with "started" (past).

</details>

### GATE 2026 Q6 (2 marks)

"My friend and I parted __ the door __ the cabin that I had rented __ the night."
(A) at; of; for (B) for; at; of (C) of; for; in (D) in; of; for

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (A)**

**at** the door (a point), the door **of** the cabin (part of it), rented **for** the night (a duration).

</details>

## Test yourself (B): predicted 2027-style questions

**F1.** "By the time the guests arrived, we __________ the hall."
(A) decorated (B) have decorated (C) had decorated (D) were decorating

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (C)**

The decorating was completed before another past event (the arrival), so it takes the past perfect.

</details>

**F2.** "Neither the manager nor the employees __________ aware of the change."
(A) was (B) were (C) is (D) has been

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (B)**

The verb agrees with the nearer subject, "employees", which is plural.

</details>

**F3.** "She has been working here __________ 2019."
(A) for (B) since (C) from (D) by

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (B)**

2019 is a starting point. You'd use "for" with a duration, such as "for five years".

</details>

**F4.** "He is __________ honest man and __________ asset to the team."
(A) a; an (B) an; an (C) an; a (D) the; a

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (B)**

"Honest" begins with a vowel sound (the h is silent), and "asset" begins with a vowel.

</details>

**F5.** "Each of the reports __________ been reviewed."
(A) have (B) has (C) are (D) were

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (B)**

The subject is "each", which is singular.

</details>
