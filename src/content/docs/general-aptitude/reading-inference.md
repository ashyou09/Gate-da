**Why it matters:** 2024 Q6 (what follows from a short passage) and 2026 Q5 (a counterfactual sentence) are 1–2 mark questions where the answer is the option that **must** be true given **only** the text. The skill is refusing to add outside knowledge.

## Topic map

<div class="mindmap">

- **Inference**
  - "Can be inferred" = must be true from the text alone
  - Eliminate
    - extreme words (all, never, always, essential)
    - outside knowledge, reversed cause and effect
  - Conditionals
    - "if X had happened, Y would have" → X did not happen
  - Argument parts
    - premise, conclusion, assumption
  - Narrative sequencing
    - order sentences by connectives and pronouns

</div>

## 3.1 What "inferred" means

An option is a valid **inference** if it **must** be true whenever the passage is true. Anything that is merely *plausible*, *likely* or *true in real life* is not enough.

**Elimination checklist:**

1. **Extreme words** (*all, none, always, never, only, essential, proves*): the text rarely supports them. Reject unless it says so explicitly.
2. **Outside knowledge:** if you need facts not in the passage, reject the option.
3. **Scope shift:** the passage says "some people", the option says "all humans". Reject.
4. **Reversed cause and effect.** Reject.
5. The surviving option usually **restates** the passage in more general words.

## 3.2 Counterfactual conditionals

<div class="formula">

| Sentence | What it tells you |
|---|---|
| If it **had rained**, the match **would have been** cancelled. | It did **not** rain, and the match was **not** cancelled. |
| If I **were** taller, I **would** play basketball. | I'm not tall (now). |
| If it rains, the match **will** be cancelled. | Nothing about whether it rains. A real possibility. |

</div>

A third-conditional sentence asserts that its **condition was false**. Its consequence is only hypothetical: it tells you nothing about what actually happened later.

## 3.3 Assumption vs conclusion

- A **premise** is a stated fact.
- A **conclusion** is the claim the passage argues for.
- An **assumption** is an **unstated** premise that the conclusion needs. Negate the candidate: if the argument collapses, it was an assumption.

## 3.4 Narrative sequencing (jumbled sentences)

- Find the **opener**: it introduces the topic and has no pronoun pointing back ("This", "He", "However").
- Pair the **linked sentences**: a pronoun follows its noun, "however" follows the idea it contrasts, "therefore" follows its cause.
- Check the time order: first, then, finally.

---

## Summary: important points

<div class="formula">

1. An inference must be true from the text alone.
2. Reject options with extreme words, outside facts, a wider scope or reversed causality.
3. *If … had …, would have …* means the condition was false.
4. An assumption is an unstated premise. The negation test: if the argument fails without it, it's an assumption.
5. Jumbled sentences: opener first, then follow the pronouns and connectives.

</div>

## Test yourself (A): previous-year questions

### GATE 2024 Q6 (2 marks)

"Thousands of years ago, some people began dairy farming. This coincided with a number of mutations in a particular gene that resulted in these people developing the ability to digest dairy milk." What can be inferred?
(A) All human beings can digest dairy milk. (B) No human being can digest dairy milk. (C) Digestion of dairy milk is essential for human beings. (D) In human beings, digestion of dairy milk resulted from a mutated gene.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (D)**

The passage says the mutations **resulted in** the ability to digest milk. (A) and (B) are extreme claims about all humans, and (C) introduces "essential", which the text never says.

</details>

### GATE 2026 Q5 (1 mark)

"If his latest movie had been a commercial success, the actor would have made enough money to sponsor his next movie." What must be true?
(A) The actor will certainly sponsor his next movie. (B) His latest movie was a commercial success. (C) The actor made enough money from his latest movie. (D) His latest movie was not commercially successful.

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (D)**

It's a third conditional, so the condition (commercial success) did not happen. (A), (B) and (C) assert the hypothetical as if it were fact.

</details>

## Test yourself (B): predicted 2027-style questions

**F1.** "Some students who attended the workshop scored above 90 in the test." Which **must** be true?
(A) All students above 90 attended the workshop (B) The workshop caused high scores (C) At least one workshop attendee scored above 90 (D) Students who skipped the workshop scored below 90

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (C)**

"Some" means at least one. Each of the others claims more than the sentence says.

</details>

**F2.** "If the bridge had been inspected, the crack would have been found." Which follows?
(A) The crack was found (B) The bridge was not inspected (C) The bridge will be inspected (D) There is no crack

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (B)**

It's a counterfactual, so the condition was false.

</details>

**F3.** "The city installed more streetlights, so crime will fall." Which is an assumption of this argument?
(A) Crime was high last year (B) Better lighting deters crime (C) Streetlights are expensive (D) Police patrols will increase

<details>
<summary>Show solution</summary>

**Answer: (B)**

Negate it ("lighting does not deter crime") and the conclusion has no support.

</details>
